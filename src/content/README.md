# SAWAAL content guide

All lessons are data in `src/content/`. Components render them.

## Voice
- Plain, conversational Indian English. Short sentences. No jargon without a gloss.
- Never shame a learner for a choice. Every `response` is kind, including for `risky` choices.
- Max 3 choices. One idea per screen. Insight is ONE memorable sentence.
- Don't push parties, ideologies or religious positions. Examine claims and practices, not identities.
- Respect religious and cultural identity; it is fine to examine a testable claim critically.

## Credibility
- `fictionalScenario: true` always. People and messages in scenes are invented; use neutral Indian first names (Asha, Ravi, Meera, Imran, Kiran, Salma, Tara, Dev).
- Never invent studies, statistics, legal provisions, quotations or percentages. If you don't know a real number, don't use a number.
- Sources must be real and verifiable. Prefer official/primary sources: legislative.gov.in (Constitution), indiacode.nic.in (Acts), eci.gov.in, rbi.org.in, rbikehtahai.rbi.org.in, cybercrime.gov.in, mohfw.gov.in, who.int, pib.gov.in, ncsc.nic.in, ncw.nic.in, cic.gov.in (RTI), ascionline.in, nih.gov. If unsure of a deep URL, link to the organisation's homepage and name the document in the label.
- `claimKind` describes the lesson's core teaching: `fact` (settled law/science), `hypothesis`, `opinion` (contested policy), `fiction`.
- `reviewedOn` is today's date in ISO.

## Languages
- `en` required. `hi` (Hindi, Devanagari) and `gu` (Gujarati) required for all content strings.
- Keep translations simple and natural, not literal. Keep numbers, ₹ and brand-like words (OTP, WhatsApp) as-is.
